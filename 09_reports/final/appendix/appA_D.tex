\section{Metric Definitions}
\label{app:metrics}
Let $y_t$ be actual demand, $\hat y_t$ the forecast, $n$ the test size. MAE
$=n^{-1}\sum|y_t-\hat y_t|$. With seasonal period $m=7$ and training-only
seasonal-naive denominator $Q=(T-m)^{-1}\sum_{t=m+1}^{T}|y_t-y_{t-m}|$ over the
frozen train window, MASE $=(n^{-1}\sum|y_t-\hat y_t|)/Q$
\citep{hyndman2006}. RMSSE replaces absolute errors with squared ones under
the root. WAPE (model level) is the mean of per-series ratios
$\sum|y-\hat y|/\sum|y|$; WAPE (archetype level) is the pooled ratio
$\sum_{\text{cell}}|y-\hat y|/\sum_{\text{cell}}|y|$, adopted after a
mean-of-ratios computation exploded to $\sim2\times10^9$ on
near-zero-test-demand series. sMAPE $=100\,n^{-1}\sum 2|y-\hat y|/(|y|+|\hat
y|)$, reported with its zero-demand distortions documented. Zero denominators
stay honest NaNs ($n_{\text{NaN}}=0$ everywhere); no epsilon guards.

\section{Statistical Methodology}
\label{app:stats}
For each model pair, paired errors $d_i=|e^A_i|-|e^B_i|$ ($n=112{,}000$):
Wilcoxon signed-rank (normal approximation) and Diebold--Mariano
\citep{diebold1995} tests, Holm step-down correction \citep{holm1979} across
all 91 pairs, 500-replication bootstrap percentile CIs for mean $d$, Cohen
paired $d_z=\bar d/s_d$, and Wilcoxon rank-biserial $r$. ``A better'' in the
raw file means $\bar d<0$; the report's verdicts additionally require material
$|d_z|$ and interval support. Because nominal paired observations repeat across series, origins, and forecast horizons, Diebold--Mariano tests and bootstrap confidence intervals inherit this dependence structure; they are therefore interpreted alongside effect sizes rather than as evidence from independent observations. The MA--SES M5 disagreement (Wilcoxon
significant, DM Holm $p=0.113$) is the worked example of restraint.
