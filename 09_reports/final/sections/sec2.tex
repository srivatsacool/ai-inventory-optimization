\section{Research Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{End-to-end research workflow}
\label{sec:workflow}
The study is organized as a notebook sequence: a research map (00) states the
question; environment control (01) fixes seeds and software; acquisition and
audit notebooks (02a, 02b) verify both datasets; exploratory analysis (03)
characterizes demand before model selection; experimental design (04) locks
the protocol; a teaching notebook (05a) sets out time-series vocabulary; then
the model rungs run in order (baselines in 05b, exponential smoothing in 06,
Croston specialists in 06c, ARIMA/SARIMA in 07, LSTM in 08). A subsequent
hardening pass corrected calendar, validation, comparability, metric, and
policy issues and froze the evidence; see \cref{sec:hardening}. Each
notebook's role in the report is recorded in the traceability matrix
(\texttt{traceability/notebook\_traceability.csv}).

\subsection{Data acquisition and audit}
\label{sec:acquisition}
M5 source files (\texttt{sales\_train\_evaluation.csv}, \texttt{calendar.csv})
were audited for dimensions (30,490 item-store series; day columns d\_1 onward
with verified date-index alignment), and the Store file (\texttt{train.csv})
was verified as a complete panel: every store-item-date combination present,
no duplicates, a single zero-sales entry. Both datasets were reindexed onto
one common calendar so that cross-dataset comparison does not confound model
effects with differences in history length.

\subsection{Temporal experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\Cref{tab:protocol} states the frozen design. Splits are time-based only.
Training covers 2013-01-01 to 2015-10-31 (1,034 days); validation 2015-11-01
to 2016-02-29 (121 days, leap day included after the hardening correction);
testing 2016-03-01 to 2016-05-22 (83 days). Forecasts span $h=28$ days from
each of 8 weekly rolling origins under an expanding window, with each model
restricted strictly to history preceding each origin (asserted in code). All
model selection (averaging windows, smoothing weights, ARIMA orders, Croston
parameters, LSTM stopping) uses validation data only; the test window is used
once for final scoring. The global seed is 42 throughout.

\subsection{Series selection}
\label{sec:selection}
Store demand uses all 500 series, so no sampling was required. M5 contributes
a stratified sample of 500 (recorded in
\texttt{05\_experiments/m5\_series\_selection.json}) covering all demand
archetypes and departments. Random sampling risked omitting rare but
substantively important demand shapes, while full-catalog deep learning was
computationally infeasible. Representativeness is documented in
\Cref{fig:representativeness}.

\subsection{Demand characterization and archetypes}
\label{sec:archetypes-method}
Following the Syntetos-Boylan tradition \citep{syntetos2005}, intermittency is
characterized per series by average demand interval (ADI) and squared
coefficient of variation (CV$^2$). The resulting quadrants are labeled here as
Smooth, Intermittent, Erratic/Variable, and Lumpy/Highly Intermittent, with an
additional Moderate band; volume terciles provide a second axis. M5 archetype
counts are 194 highly intermittent, 199 intermittent, 26 moderate, 80
variable, and 1 smooth. The single smooth series is reported for completeness
but supports no inference (\cref{sec:smooth-cap}).

\subsection{Model-ladder design}
\label{sec:ladder}
Models are ordered by complexity while evaluation is held constant: baselines
(Naive, Seasonal Naive, Moving Average) define the threshold any method must
clear; SES, DES (Holt), and additive TES add memory for level, trend, and
seasonality; ARIMA adds autocorrelation modeling and SARIMA seasonal
autocorrelation; the Croston family specializes in intermittent demand; the
global LSTM learns nonlinear patterns pooled across all series of a dataset.
Complexity is the independent variable; whether it produces value is the
empirical question. \Cref{fig:ladder} summarizes the arrangement.

\begin{figure}[H]

increases with it is tested in \cref{sec:findings}. Teaching figure from

\subsection{Forecasting models}
\label{sec:forecast-models}
Naive carries the last observed day forward 28 days. Seasonal Naive repeats
the last seven observed days (an early implementation that reached beyond the
seasonal window was identified and corrected to use observed values only).
Moving Average uses validation-selected global windows (M5: 14 days; Store: 7
days; validation MAE 0.9206 and 7.4057 respectively). SES tracks a moving
level ($\alpha=0.1$), DES adds trend ($\alpha=\beta=0.2$), and additive TES
adds a weekly seasonal component ($0.3/0.3/0.3$); all use transparent
implementations with validation-grid selection. ARIMA(1,1,0) runs on both
datasets with a sparse-data pre-check (series with more than 85\% zeros fall
back to Naive rather than fitting noise); per-series order search was
rejected as overfitting-prone and computationally out of scope.
SARIMA(1,1,0)(0,1,1)$_7$ runs Store-only, since seasonal differencing of
predominantly zero series lacks methodological justification and the Croston
family already covers the M5 specialty. Croston, SBA, and TSB run M5-only
(validation parameters 0.1, 0.5, and 0.1--0.3), as flat intermittent-demand
forecasts have no applicability to dense data. The LSTM is one global network
per dataset with a 28-day lookback, per-series history-only scaling, and
chronological per-series validation (rebuilt during hardening to remove
overlapping-window leakage), seeded at 42. Full configurations are in
\cref{app:configs}.

\subsection{Forecast evaluation metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
Headline accuracy is mean absolute error (MAE); the primary scale-free metric
is MASE with seasonal period $m=7$ (the weekly retail rhythm shown in
\cref{fig:weekly}), whose denominator is the in-sample one-step seasonal-naive
error computed on training data only. Validation and test data never enter
the denominator. RMSE, RMSSE, WAPE, and sMAPE are reported alongside; no
metric is selected after seeing results. Two conventions matter for
interpretation. First, zero denominators (constant training history) are left
as honest missing values rather than epsilon-adjusted (no such cases
occurred: $n_{\text{NaN}}=0$ throughout). Second, archetype-level WAPE and
sMAPE are pooled ratios over each cell, because averaging per-series ratios
allows a single near-zero-demand series to dominate the mean; model-level
WAPE columns remain per-series means and were verified consistent across
files. sMAPE is reported with its known distortions on near-zero demand
documented. Definitions are formalized in \cref{app:metrics}.

\subsection{Statistical comparison}
\label{sec:stats-method}
Ninety-one paired comparisons (55 M5 and 36 Store) combine the Wilcoxon
signed-rank test (normal approximation) with the Diebold-Mariano test
\citep{diebold1995}, Holm correction \citep{holm1979} for multiplicity,
500-replication bootstrap confidence intervals \citep{efron1993}, paired
Cohen $d_z$, and the Wilcoxon rank-biserial $r$. With 112,000 paired errors,
tests have high power to detect small differences, so each $p$-value is
reported together with an effect size. A qualification on the observation
count is required: the 112,000 paired errors per comparison arise from 500
series observed across 8 rolling origins and 28 horizon steps, with
overlapping estimation windows. They are repeated, dependent observations, not
112,000 independent draws, and the nominal count should not be read as an
effective independent sample size. The paired tests were preregistered on
this structure and remain the reported inference; series-level or
block-bootstrap inference that respects the dependence explicitly is
identified as a robustness extension in \cref{sec:l-future}. Methodological
detail is in \cref{app:stats}.

\subsection{Inventory policy}
\label{sec:policy}
All forecasts pass through one shared ordering implementation
(\texttt{11\_src/inventory\_policy.py}), identical for every model by
construction: a daily-review order-up-to (base-stock) policy with lost sales.
For each series-origin instance, safety stock equals $z$ times the
forecast-error standard deviation (floored) times $\sqrt{L}$; the order-up-to
level is the sum of the next $L$ forecasts plus safety stock; unmet demand is
lost at penalty $P$ per unit; end-of-day stock accrues holding cost $H$ per
unit. The default ranking policy is $L=7$, 95\% service target ($z\approx
1.645$), $H=1$, $P=5$. Total cost decomposes into holding and stockout
components, with service level, average inventory, and reorder frequency as
diagnostics. \Cref{fig:invlink} illustrates how forecasts enter ordering
decisions.

\subsection{Robustness and sensitivity analysis}
\label{sec:sens-method}
Headline rankings use the default policy. Their stability is then tested on a
27-policy grid ($L \in \{3,7,14\}$, service $\in \{90\%,95\%,99\%\}$,
$P \in \{3,5,10\}$, $H=1$), replaying every model-dataset through 540 costing
runs. A model ranked first across nearly all policies is described as robust;
one whose rank moves with the policy is described as policy-sensitive.
Per-archetype M5 costing adds the demand-structure dimension.

\subsection{Convergence and feasibility}
\label{sec:conv-method}
Every ARIMA/SARIMA estimation is logged per series-origin
(\texttt{convergence\_details\_*.csv}), with a scope column separating the
exploratory 100-series SARIMA subset from the primary full-500 run. A 16-fit
feasibility probe (0.62\,s per fit) preceded the 4,000-fit full-500 SARIMA run
(2,382\,s; 4,000 of 4,000 successful). The contrast between complete
convergence on dense data and partial pre-check fallback on sparse data is
itself an empirical result about estimation feasibility under demand
structure.

\subsection{Validation and reproducibility}
\label{sec:validation}
An independent validator re-checks row counts (112,000 per model-dataset),
duplicate keys (zero), origin coverage (1--8), horizon length (28 throughout),
test-window containment, forecast finiteness and non-negativity, and
cross-family actuals identity (maximum spread 0.0): 0 FAIL
(\texttt{06\_results/validation\_report.json}). Seeds (42), manifests, run
logs, and a pre-hardening backup make each step replayable; provenance is
inventoried in \cref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Research hardening and final evidence freeze}
\label{sec:hardening}
Before reporting, the evidence passed a hardening audit that corrected the
calendar (leap day 2016-02-29 assigned to validation, giving 1,034/121/83),
revalidated the LSTM on chronological per-series splits, fixed a Seasonal
Naive lookahead, unified all models onto one inventory module, corrected MASE
grouping and Wilcoxon effect sizes, pooled archetype WAPE/sMAPE, replaced the
SARIMA subset with the full-500 run as the primary result, and repaired
identifiers. Pre-hardening originals are preserved untouched in
\texttt{06\_results/\_pre\_hardening\_backup/} and are not report sources.
The freeze (commits 53c8343 through edb42ad) generated the number sheet,
figure and table audits, and claim traceability programmatically; no headline
number in this report was entered by hand. Hardening is documented here to
establish the provenance of the evidence.
