\section{Learning, Contribution and Practical Implications}
\label{sec:contrib}

\subsection{Empirical contribution}
\label{sec:c-emp}
The study demonstrates the forecast--decision gap in both directions inside one
controlled design: alignment on intermittent M5 demand, divergence on dense
Store demand, with the paradoxical Store podium (MA $>$ SES $>$ SNaive $>$
LSTM) as its sharpest exhibit. It also maps exactly where sophistication pays
(LSTM accuracy everywhere; LSTM cost on sparse demand) and where it bills
without benefit (TES/DES; ARIMA on dense demand).

\subsection{Methodological contribution}
\label{sec:c-meth}
Three reusable instruments: (1) a shared-policy evaluation harness that makes
cross-model inventory comparison fair by construction --- one module, every
model, no per-model tuning; (2) a leakage-safe rolling protocol (history
strictly before origin, validation-only selection, honest-NaN scale-free
metrics with pooled archetype ratios); (3) sensitivity-as-verdict: headline
rankings are published with their 27-policy stability record, separating
bankable conclusions from conditional ones.

\subsection{Analytical contribution}
\label{sec:c-anal}
Ninety-one Holm-corrected paired comparisons with effect sizes and bootstrap
intervals, reported with the explicit doctrine that $p$-values never travel
without $d_z$. The MA--SES ``honest null'' shows the machinery can exonerate
as well as convict.

\subsection{Inventory-management implications}
\label{sec:c-inv}
For intermittent catalogs: invest in learned or specialist forecasting (LSTM,
TSB/SBA) and expect the forecast win to reach the shelf. For dense,
fast-moving catalogs: pilot simple averaging methods against the neural option
\emph{under your own cost ratio} before committing engineering budget; the
cheapest stocker may already be in production. In both worlds, distrust any
vendor leaderboard that stops at accuracy.

\subsection{Model-selection implications}
\label{sec:c-select}
Select conditional on three answers: What is my demand structure (sparse or
dense)? What is my cost asymmetry ($P/H$)? What is my lead time? The report's
tables answer the rest: sparse + any reasonable policy $\rightarrow$ LSTM;
dense + default policy $\rightarrow$ Moving Average with SES as backup; dense
+ short-lead/high-penalty $\rightarrow$ re-run, leadership is fluid.

\subsection{Reproducibility contribution}
\label{sec:c-repro}
The frozen package (number sheet, audits, traceability, validation log with 0
FAIL, pre-hardening backup, seeds, manifests) means any number in this report
resolves to a source file in one lookup. Reproducibility here is not a claim
but an address book (\cref{app:repro}).

\subsection{Research-hardening lessons}
\label{sec:c-harden}
The most expensive errors were methodological, not algorithmic: an unassigned
leap day, overlapping validation windows, a subset presented at full weight, a
mean-of-ratios that exploded to $10^9$. Each was caught by an audit that
assumed nothing --- row counts, date containment, denominator discipline,
population labels. The lesson for any analytics team: validate the experiment
with the same paranoia you apply to the model, because the experiment is what
you actually conclude from.
