\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Forecast accuracy is not decision quality}
\label{sec:d-acc-dec}
The executive summary of this entire report is one asymmetry. Accuracy metrics
are symmetric, per-unit, and policy-free; inventory costs are asymmetric,
thresholded, and policy-defined. Safety stock transforms error \emph{volatility}
into money; the order-up-to rule transforms error \emph{bias} into money; the
$P/H$ ratio decides the exchange rate. Any evaluation that stops at the
forecast has measured the ingredient, not the dish.

\subsection{Why LSTM wins forecasting but not always inventory}
\label{sec:d-lstm}
The global LSTM learns shapes no per-series linear model can: zero-inflated
regimes, cross-item weekly patterns, nonlinear rebounds. That buys the best
point forecasts on both datasets. But inventory does not buy point forecasts;
it buys a distribution summary (error volatility for safety stock) plus a level
(the next-$L$ sum). On dense Store demand, the LSTM's sharpness comes with
occasional confident under-predictions --- cheap under MAE, ruinous at $P=5$.
The moving average, by construction, rarely under-shoots violently. Precision
without caution is a fast car with weak brakes: brilliant on the track (error
metrics), expensive in traffic (asymmetric costs).

\subsection{Why simple models remain competitive}
\label{sec:d-simple}
Moving Average and SES do two things right. First, averaging is the optimal
response to noise-dominated demand: most daily variation is unpredictable, and
the mean of recent days is the minimum-variance guess. Second, their errors
are symmetric and stable, which is exactly what safety-stock formulas assume.
Simplicity aligns with the policy's assumptions; sophistication violates them
profitably only when the demand signal is strong enough to repay the violation
--- true on sparse M5, false on dense Store.

\subsection{Demand structure and model suitability}
\label{sec:d-structure}
Intermittency decides which machinery is legitimate. Where zeros dominate,
refuse-to-fit-zeros methods (Croston family, Naive-fallback ARIMA) and
cross-series learners (LSTM) win; linear autoregression starves. Where demand
is dense and weekly, seasonal structure (SARIMA, Seasonal Naive) pays and
trend machinery (DES/TES) overfits noise into inventory. The archetype tables
are the suitability map; the Store--M5 contrast is its validation.

\subsection{Inventory policy as the effective loss function}
\label{sec:d-policy-loss}
A useful reframe: the choice of $(L, z, P/H)$ \emph{is} the choice of loss
function, and forecasters are implicitly optimized for the wrong one (symmetric
error). The sensitivity grid makes this visible: move $P$ from 3 to 10 and the
optimal forecaster changes. Practitioners should therefore select models
\emph{conditional} on their policy, and researchers reporting accuracy-only
leaderboards should attach a warning label.

\subsection{Statistical significance vs.\ practical importance}
\label{sec:d-sig}
At $n=112{,}000$, significance is nearly free and therefore nearly meaningless
alone. The M5 LSTM--TSB pair ($p \approx 2\times10^{-10}$, $d_z=-0.031$) would
headline a naive report as ``highly significant superiority''; the honest
headline is ``indistinguishable for any business purpose.'' Effect sizes,
intervals, and the MA--SES disagreement between tests are the report's immune
system against $p$-value theater.

\subsection{Complexity vs.\ value}
\label{sec:d-complex}
Capability and value parted ways. The ladder's complexity order (Naive $<$ MA
$<$ SES $<$ DES $<$ TES $<$ ARIMA $<$ SARIMA $<$ LSTM) predicts neither the
accuracy order nor the cost order. The only complexity that paid was the
neural kind, and only in accuracy everywhere and cost in one world. Budget
accordingly: a moving average costs nothing to run and nearly wins; an LSTM
costs real engineering and wins sometimes.

\subsection{Robust vs.\ fragile conclusions}
\label{sec:d-robust}
Robustness is a property of the \emph{conclusion under policy variation}, not
of the model. M5-LSTM is robust (25/27, losses confined to one explicable
corner). Store-MA is fragile (9/27) --- and so is every Store alternative.
Fragile does not mean wrong: under the stated default policy MA genuinely wins.
It means conditional: change the lead time or cost ratio and re-run the
comparison before spending money.

\subsection{What can and cannot be generalized}
\label{sec:d-general}
Transferable: the forecast--decision gap mechanism, the significance-vs-size
discipline, the sensitivity-as-verdict method, LSTM forecast superiority on
both structures, TES/DES failure modes. Not transferable: the specific winner
of any board (policy- and structure-contingent), anything about the Smooth
archetype ($n=1$), anything about LLMs (unexecuted), anything beyond $h=28$ or
univariate demand. The contribution is a map with marked hazards, not a
universal champion.
